Voting Rights in America

What constitutional amendment was passed after the civil war that expanded voting rights?

The 15th Amendment, 1870, ensured that voting rights cannot be denied "on account of race, color, or previous conditon of servitude." This amendment was passed following the civil war so that now free population of black men can now participate in politics.

What loopholes did certain states use to exclude black people from voting post 15th amendment?

Post reconstrction in 1877, white supremacist lawmakers in the southern US found ways to exclude black voters from participating. They implemented poll taxes that voters had to pay 2 years before the election or they couldn't vote. The lawmakers knew that black southerners made up the poorest population so they couldn't pay it. Another barrier they created was literacy tests; since most black southerners were ex-enslaved people, they could not read.

When did women get the right to vote?

On August 8th 1920, the 19th amendment was ratifed, which "prohibted the denial of votes based on sex." This came after many years of women's sufferage movments and even after the 19th amendment was passed, the barriers created by lawmakers in the south to exclude minorities still remained.

What did the 24th amendment do?

The 24th amendment, ratified in 1964, was created to end the discrimatory practices that disenfranchised low-income black voters.
